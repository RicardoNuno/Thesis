# nesi2026: Objectively measured smartphone pickups among adolescents: Associations with daily positive and negative affect and mindfulness

**Authors / venue / year:** Nesi, Burnell, Fox, … Prinstein & Telzer / Psychology of Popular Media 15(1), 1–10 / 2026 (online 2025)
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1037/ppm0000601 (code: https://osf.io/swkc9)
**Relevance:** medium

_Read from the accepted manuscript; page numbers below are manuscript pages, not journal pages._

## Summary (3 sentences)

135 rural North Carolina adolescents (iPhone users) took part in a 14-day EMA study, rating their affect three times a day and uploading a daily screenshot of the previous day's iOS Screen Time pickups. Teens who picked up their phones more on average had more variable negative affect, and on days after lower-than-usual positive affect, teens low in trait mindfulness picked up their phones more. Pickups did not predict same-day or next-day affect, so the data point to mood driving checking rather than checking harming mood.

## Method

- N = 135 (of 217 in the EMA, 178 iPhone users, 152 with ≥ 1 valid screenshot; kept those with ≥ 3 days). Mean age 15.46 (14–17), 62% female, 34% Hispanic, 33% White, 24% Black; low–medium income. May–September 2020 (COVID).
- Part of a longitudinal school study (waves 2016–2019); mindfulness from Wave 4 (CAMM, 10 items, α = .91).
- EMA via ExpiWell, 3 random prompts/day (9–13, 13–17, 17–22 h) plus an evening diary. Modified PANAS "right now": PA = happy, calm (α = .76); NA = 8 items (α = .83). Daily means; variability = SD over the EMA period.
- Pickups: daily screenshot of Screen Time → See All Activity → Pickups (total for the previous day, plus "first app used after pickup"). Mean 10.77 valid days per person.
- Mplus 8.7. Person-level regressions (pickups → affect variability) and multilevel models with within/between split: concurrent, pickups → next-day affect, affect → next-day pickups; mindfulness as moderator. Only results robust to sensitivity checks (N = 152; covariates) were interpreted.

## Results / evidence

- 112.63 pickups/day (SD 68.66).
- First app after pickup (59 apps seen): Snapchat 47%, Instagram 12%, Messages 12%, TikTok 7%, Safari 3%, YouTube 3%.
- Average pickups → NA variability, β = .20, p = .011. Pickups → PA variability β = .17 but not robust (p = .079 with covariates). Mindfulness did not moderate either.
- Concurrent: no within- or between-person link between pickups and affect.
- Pickups → next-day affect: nothing (β ≈ .03).
- PA → next-day pickups: β = −.08, p = .005; moderated by mindfulness. Low mindfulness: b = −13.12 pickups per 1-point drop in PA, β = −.15, p < .001; high mindfulness: n.s. (p = .362). NA → next-day pickups: n.s.
- Effects are small (authors' words).

## Strengths / limitations

- Strength: logged pickups (not self-report) over 14 days with EMA, so within-person and both temporal directions can be tested.
- Strength: separates positive and negative affect and looks at variability, not just level; conservative rule of only interpreting robust results; code on OSF.
- Limitation: daily totals only; no timing of pickups, so no link to the moment of checking. Authors say the daily timescale may be wrong.
- Limitation: no reason for each pickup (notification vs self-initiated) and no activity after it, beyond the first app.
- Limitation: iPhone only, and screenshots depend on teens uploading them correctly (attrition from 217 to 135; analytic sample more female and happier).
- Limitation: COVID period; PA measured with only 2 items; small sample.
- Limitation: text says mindfulness is linked to *greater* affect variability, but Table 1 (r = −.32, −.36) and Table 2 (β = −.30, −.34) show the opposite. Table 5 Model 2 SE (.07 for b = −4.78) looks like a typo.

## How it relates to my thesis

Gives a recent logged baseline for checking in teens (~113 pickups/day), to set beside heitmayer2021 and wilcockson2018, and another case of iOS Screen Time as a data source (ohme2021). It also shows the limits of that source: daily totals from screenshots cannot say when or why a pickup happened, which is what my logging should capture (e.g. notification-triggered vs self-initiated, as in heitmayer2021). The direction of the effect matters for framing: low mood → more checking, not checking → low mood, so the thesis should not assume each pickup is harmful; awareness of checking (mindfulness moderates the link) is a more defensible angle.
