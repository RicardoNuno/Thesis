# ellis2019: Do smartphone usage scales predict behavior?

**Authors / venue / year:** Ellis, Davidson, Shaw & Geyer / International Journal of Human-Computer Studies 130, 86–92 / 2019
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2019.05.004 (accepted manuscript: https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/133310/)
**Relevance:** high

## Summary (3 sentences)

238 iPhone users gave single estimates of daily screen time, pick-ups and notifications, filled in ten popular smartphone use / "addiction" scales, then copied one week of Apple Screen Time data (time, pick-ups, notifications). Every scale correlated only weakly with logged behaviour (r = .13–.40 with time, ≤ .31 with pick-ups), and none beat a single one-question estimate; the best scales were the SUQ general and absent-minded scales, which frame use as habit and inattention rather than addiction. Clustering people into high/low users from scales vs from logs agreed for only 57% of behavioural high users (32% with three clusters), so the scales cannot be used as a proxy for actual use.

## Method

- N = 238 (124 F, mean age 31.9, SD 11.2); 77% via Prolific, 23% from Lancaster, Bath, Lincoln. iPhone 5+ on latest iOS for ≥ 1 week.
- Order: single estimates (daily time, notifications, pick-ups; told not to look at the phone) → ten scales in random order → transcribe Screen Time data for the past week.
- Scales: MPPUS, NMP-Q (yildirim2015), Extended Self, Attachment, SAS (33-item, kwon2013sas), SABAS, PMPUQ (short 15-item), MTUAS smartphone subscale, SUQ-G and SUQ-A.
- Behaviour = daily averages over 6 complete days (collection day dropped); no weekday differences (all p > .2).
- Pearson correlations; "overall performance" = mean r across the three logged measures; k-means (k = 2, then k = 3) on logs only vs on scales only, then compare who lands in the high cluster.

## Results / evidence

- Logged: 232.7 min/day (SD 119.4), 85.8 pick-ups (SD 53.3), 90.1 notifications (SD 88.9). Estimated: 226.6 min, 45.7 pick-ups, 39.1 notifications.
- Time: all scales correlate positively, r = .13–.40 (SAS highest, .40); single time estimate r = .48.
- Pick-ups: SUQ-G .31, SUQ-A .30, SAS .22, MPPUS .18, MTUAS .15; Extended Self, Attachment, SABAS n.s.; single pick-up estimate r = .23 (Table 3; the text says .32, which is actually the notification estimate's r with pick-ups).
- Notifications: single estimate r = .53; scales ≤ .28.
- Mean r across the three measures: notification estimate .33 and time estimate .33 best, then SUQ-G .31, SUQ-A .29.
- Scales correlate strongly with each other (MPPUS–SAS .82, NS–SAS .75): many measure nearly the same thing.
- Age correlates negatively with all logged measures (time −.20, pick-ups −.32, notifications −.35).
- Pick-ups and notifications correlate r = .66; time and pick-ups r = .39.
- k = 2 on logs: 92 high, 146 low; only 52/92 (56.5%) also high from scales. k = 3: 10/31 (32.3%).
- Authors' reading: scales may overlap more with anxiety/depression wording than with use.

## Strengths / limitations

- Strength: much larger sample than earlier validation studies (andrews2015 n = 23); ten scales side by side against the same logs.
- Strength: includes pick-ups, not only time, so it tests the "checking" behaviour the addiction scales claim to capture.
- Limitation: Screen Time is visible to users, so estimates may be better than in andrews2015 (pick-up estimate now r = .23 vs .11 there); participants only asked not to look.
- Limitation: data copied by hand from the Screen Time screen; daily averages only, no session-level or hourly data; iPhone only.
- Limitation: averaging r across time, pick-ups and notifications mixes different constructs; it rewards the notification estimate for matching its own counterpart.
- Limitation: reporting errors: NMP-Q listed as 20 items in methods but 11 in Table 1; PEst r with pick-ups given as .32 in text vs .23 in Table 3; MTUAS range 9–90 but reported as a mean (6.24); cites the SAS-SV paper for the 33-item SAS. Calls itself "the first" comparison despite citing earlier ones.
- Limitation: correlations only, no outcome (wellbeing) measure, so it cannot say which measure matters more for harm.

## How it relates to my thesis

The strongest evidence so far that self-report scales should not stand in for logged use: SAS, MPPUS and NMP-Q (all on my reading list) explain at most ~16% of the variance in screen time and ~5% in pick-ups. Backs andrews2015 at scale (pick-ups ~86/day, almost identical) and the panova2018 / james2023 critique of the addiction framing; the habit/inattention scales (SUQ) did best, which fits the checking-habit view (oulasvirta2012, heitmayer2021). For the prototype: log time, pick-ups and notifications directly (all available on Android), and if I use a questionnaire, treat it as measuring the user's perception, not their behaviour; SUQ-A is the better candidate if one is needed. The pick-up / notification correlation (.66) and near 1:1 ratio fit mehrotra2016 / pielot2014 on notifications prompting use, but heitmayer2021 shows most pick-ups are self-initiated, so correlation here is not cause. Follow-up to read only if needed: Ellis 2019 CHB ("Are smartphones really that bad?").
