# tng2024: Nuanced relationships between indices of smartphone use and psychological distress: distinguishing problematic smartphone use, phone checking, and screen time

**Authors / venue / year:** Tng & Yang / Behaviour & Information Technology 43(5), 956–969 / 2024 (online 2023)
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1080/0144929X.2023.2196573 (repository copy: https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/soss_research/4137)
**Relevance:** medium

## Summary (3 sentences)

364 Singaporean university students (78% female) completed a survey measuring problematic smartphone use (SAS-SV), phone-checking frequency (3 MTES items), daily screen time (1 item) and depression, anxiety and stress (DASS-21). In a structural equation model controlling for age, sex, income, neuroticism and negative affect, checking predicted all three outcomes, problematic use predicted only anxiety, and screen time predicted only depression. Framed with Kushlev & Leitao's (2020) displacement (time taken from other activities) vs interference (interruptions during activities) hypotheses: checking → interference, screen time → displacement.

## Method

- N = 364, age 18–27 (M 22.1), private university in Singapore; SGD 10 or course credit. Cross-sectional Qualtrics survey, fixed order.
- PSU: SAS-SV (kwon2013sassv), α = .86.
- Checking: 3 MTES items (how often checked for new activity, 1 = few times/day to 7 = < every 5 min; checking in spare moments; checking during conversations), α = .60.
- Screen time: one MTUAS item, 1 = 0 min to 6 = > 4 h/day.
- DASS-21 subscales as latent factors (parcels); covariates: age, sex, household income, BFI neuroticism, PANAS negative affect.
- SEM in Mplus (WLSMV); sex × index interactions as exploratory moderation.

## Results / evidence

- Adjusted model (β): checking → depression .10, anxiety .21, stress .19 (all p < .05).
- PSU → anxiety .12 (p = .028); depression −.01, stress .10 (p = .057), n.s.
- Screen time → depression .28 (p < .001); anxiety −.09, stress −.11, n.s.
- Unadjusted, PSU had large links (β .43–.56) that mostly vanished after covariates (neuroticism, negative affect).
- Sex × PSU → stress β = .32 (p = .016), stronger in females; sex did not moderate checking or screen time effects.
- Screen time item near ceiling: M = 5.08 of 6 (most people at "3–4 h" or "> 4 h").

## Strengths / limitations

- Strength: tests the three indices together and controls for neuroticism and negative affect, which removes much of the usual PSU–distress link.
- Strength: clear theoretical frame (displacement vs interference) useful for my argument.
- Limitation: **all self-report**, including checking and screen time, which andrews2015, ellis2019 and ohme2021 show people estimate badly; the authors admit this.
- Limitation: checking scale has poor reliability (α = .60) and mixes frequency with context (spare moments, conversations).
- Limitation: screen time is one capped item with a ceiling, so it barely separates heavy users.
- Limitation: cross-sectional, so no direction (distress may cause checking); effects small (β ≈ .1–.3).
- Limitation: 78% female, one university, Singapore; sex interactions underpowered (69.8–84.8% power for a medium effect; authors' own words).
- Limitation: says good fit requires non-significant χ², then reports χ²(22) = 58.7, p < .001 as "good fit"; identical fit statistics for two different models; Table 2 note lists fewer covariates than the text.

## How it relates to my thesis

Supports focusing on checking rather than screen time: of the three indices, checking frequency is the one linked to all three distress outcomes, consistent with wilcockson2018's suggestion that checking is the more meaningful habit measure. The interference vs displacement framing (Kushlev & Leitao 2020) is a useful way to argue why reducing checks may matter more than reducing minutes, and links to stothart2015 (attention cost of interruptions). But the evidence is weak for my purpose because checking was self-reported: to make the claim properly I need logged checks, which is exactly the gap my logging approach addresses. Also another case where SAS-SV links to distress mostly via neuroticism / negative affect (panova2018 critique).
