# wilcockson2019: Digital detox: The effect of smartphone abstinence on mood, anxiety, and craving

**Authors / venue / year:** Wilcockson, Osborne & Ellis / Addictive Behaviors 99, 106013 / 2019
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addbeh.2019.06.002 (corrigendum: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addbeh.2019.106265; corrected post-print: https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/c85kx)
**Relevance:** low

_Read with the 2020 corrigendum: two data columns (craving at session 1 and addiction scores) were swapped. The craving results below are the corrected ones; the original paper's numbers are wrong._

## Summary (3 sentences)

Students came to the lab four times: a week before, right before, right after a 24-hour smartphone abstinence (phone sealed in an evidence bag), and a day after. Mood and anxiety did not change across sessions, while craving rose during the abstinence and then dropped once people had their phone back; after the correction, craving right after abstinence was not higher than a normal week earlier. The authors argue that because abstinence didn't affect mood or anxiety, heavy smartphone use doesn't meet the criteria of an addiction like substance use; people crave the phone because they like using it.

## Method

- 45 participants started (33% male, mean age 22.4), Lancaster University subject pool and posters, £15; 36 completed all four sessions.
- Sessions: (1) one week before; (2) immediately before 24-h abstinence, phone placed in a tear-open evidence bag (can be opened in emergencies; none were); (3) immediately after; (4) 24 h later.
- Measures: mood (Brief Mood Introspection Scale), anxiety (STAI-6), craving (Desire for Alcohol Questionnaire adapted to smartphones, 37 items), Smartphone Addiction Inventory (SPAI) at session 1. Repeated-measures ANOVA plus Bayes factors.
- Framework: Kardefelt-Winther et al. (2017): a behavioural addiction should show withdrawal-like mood change, anxiety and craving during abstinence.

## Results / evidence

- Mood: no effect of session (p = .15, BF10 = 0.29, some evidence for no effect); slightly lower right before abstinence and higher at the end.
- Anxiety: no effect (p = .36, BF10 = 0.13).
- Craving (corrected): significant effect of session; rose from right before (M ≈ 85) to right after abstinence (M ≈ 99), then fell the next day (M ≈ 67). But session 1, a normal week, was already ≈ 94, so craving after abstinence was **not** higher than baseline (p = .38).
- Higher SPAI scores went with higher craving at every session (r ≈ .6–.7); SPAI unrelated to mood or anxiety.
- Drop-outs before session 3 had slightly higher SPAI scores (67 vs 55 mean), so the heaviest users may have quit.

## Strengths / limitations

- Strength: actual abstinence in daily life with a check on compliance (sealed bag), repeated measures before and after, Bayes factors to support null results.
- Limitation: **weak design overall**: no control group (changes could come from repeated testing or anticipation), mood and anxiety measured only in the lab before/after rather than during the abstinence, and other devices allowed.
- Limitation: only 24 hours, so it captures the very start of abstinence, not adaptation; and as with radtke2021, "how do you feel" during withdrawal is not the same as "does abstinence help".
- Limitation: small student sample, not selected for problematic use, and the heaviest users may have dropped out.
- Limitation: other devices (laptop) were allowed, so people could have compensated.
- Limitation: all self-report; SPAI is a questionnaire that barely tracks actual use (ellis2019, parry2021), as the authors note themselves.
- Limitation: the data-entry error that required a corrigendum; the uncorrected version (often cited) overstates the craving effect.

## How it relates to my thesis

Not a basis for any argument: the design (24 h, no control group, lab snapshots, laptops allowed, small non-selected sample, corrected data error) is too weak to say anything about withdrawal or the effects of abstinence. At most it can be cited as an example of how thin the detox evidence is, alongside the timing critique in radtke2021, which includes it.
