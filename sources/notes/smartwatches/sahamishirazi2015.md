# sahamishirazi2015: Assessment of Notifications on Smartwatches

**Authors / venue / year:** Sahami Shirazi & Henze / MobileHCI '15 Adjunct (workshop), 1111–1116 / 2015
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1145/2786567.2794338
**Relevance:** low

## Summary (3 sentences)

Short workshop paper asking which notifications people want on which device. An online survey (440 complete responses, May 2014, mostly students; only 4 owned a smartwatch) rated notifications as most important on smartphones, then tablets, PCs and lastly smartwatches, with the preferred categories differing per device (messengers top on phones, calendar top on watches). A one-week in-situ study with 8 Samsung Galaxy Gear users rating 5,559 watch notifications (74% WhatsApp) found calendar and VOIP (calls) notifications rated most important on the watch and messenger notifications only mid-level, so the authors argue for a cross-device notification service that routes each notification to the right device instead of mirroring everything.

## Method

- Survey: importance (1–5) of seven notification categories (messenger, VOIP, email, social, calendar, games, system) on PC, smartphone, tablet and smartwatch; which devices should receive each category; open advantages/disadvantages of smartwatches. 610 started, 440 complete (148 men, 289 women, mean age 24); English, German, Turkish.
- In-situ: Android app on the phone logging notifications shown on a paired Galaxy Gear and asking each evening to rate the day's watch notifications (1–5); 8 participants (6 men, aged 20–25), 7 days, €10.

## Results / evidence

- Survey importance by device: smartphone 3.51, tablet 3.27, PC 3.12, smartwatch 2.90 (all differences significant); on watches calendar (3.53) and messengers (3.46) rated highest; on phones messengers (4.37) then email.
- Preferred device: smartphone for every category except games (> 72%); smartwatch second only for calendar (45.4%).
- Open answers: main advantage is accessing information without using the phone; but watches cannot replace phones, and the small screen makes them "read-only".
- In-situ: 5,559 notifications from 40 apps (WhatsApp 4,126, phone 308, Facebook Messenger 187); importance on the watch: calendar 4.12, VOIP 3.66, messenger 2.98; games and other lowest.

## Strengths / limitations

- Strength: early evidence that notification value depends on the device, not just the app; a clear design argument for device-aware routing.
- Limitation: survey respondents almost never owned a smartwatch (4 of 440), so watch ratings are expectations.
- Limitation: in-situ study tiny (8 people, 1 week); the top "calendar" rating rests on just 6 notifications; end-of-day recall ratings.
- Limitation: reported spread for smartwatch importance (SD .68) is inconsistent with the other devices (SD .04, likely standard errors); no statistics for the in-situ study.

## How it relates to my thesis

Supports filtering what reaches a watch companion: most watch notifications were messaging, yet users valued them only moderately, while rarer calendar and call alerts were valued most. Together with cecchinato2017 (owners turn off notifications on the watch after the novelty) and visuri2017 (only notifications actionable on the watch should go there), it points to device-aware notification routing rather than mirroring, which also matches the selective-control theme. Evidence is weak and dated.
