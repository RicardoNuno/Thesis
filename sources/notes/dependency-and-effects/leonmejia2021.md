# leonmejia2021: A systematic review on nomophobia prevalence: Surfacing results and standard guidelines for future research

**Authors / venue / year:** León-Mejía, Gutiérrez-Ortega, Serrano-Pintado & González-Cabrera / PLOS ONE 16(5), e0250509 / 2021
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0250509
**Relevance:** medium

## Summary (3 sentences)

Systematic review of 108 nomophobia studies (to January 2020) looking at how nomophobia is measured and reported, not at pooling a number. Reported prevalence is all over the place (6% to 73% "nomophobic", 13% to 79% "at risk") because studies use different instruments, cut-offs and ways of reporting. Women and younger people seem more affected, and the authors propose reporting guidelines and percentile-based cut-offs.

## Method

- Google Scholar, Web of Science, Scopus, ProQuest, Science Direct; English and Spanish; peer-reviewed only; to 1 January 2020.
- Included studies with quantitative nomophobia data; excluded studies that were really about mobile addiction or dependence.
- No meta-analysis: studies were too different to pool. Descriptive synthesis of instruments, prevalence, gender and age differences.
- Mostly undergraduates (often health sciences); India (29) and Turkey (28) lead, then USA (9), Spain (8).

## Results / evidence

- Instruments: NMP-Q in 82 studies (15 language versions), ad hoc questionnaires in 16, other scales in 10.
- Prevalence: "at risk" 13–79%, "nomophobic" 6–73%; within nomophobic, moderate 25.7–73.3%, severe 1–87%. Mean NMP-Q scores 45.5 to 93.8.
- Gender: 24 studies found women more nomophobic, 8 found men. Women score higher on "not being able to communicate" and "giving up convenience".
- Age: more studies find younger people more affected, but age is reported in incompatible ways.
- The mild/moderate/severe NMP-Q bands come from Yildirim, Sumuer, Adnan & Yildirim (2016, Turkish college students), not from yildirim2015.

## Strengths / limitations

- Strength: makes the measurement problem explicit; useful reporting guidelines.
- Strength: separates nomophobia from mobile addiction and excludes studies that mix them.
- Limitation: no meta-analysis or quality assessment; only descriptive.
- Limitation: gender and age conclusions rest on vote counting (how many studies found X), which the authors admit is weak.
- Limitation: studies only to 2019; almamun2025 is the newer pooled estimate.

## How it relates to my thesis

The conclusions are the useful part:

- Nomophobia is often confused with mobile addiction, but they are different constructs. The thesis should keep them apart.
- Prevalence figures cannot be compared across studies; cite ranges with caution, not a single number.
- "At risk" should not be defined as "above the sample mean" ("neither theoretically nor methodologically supported"); the authors propose percentiles 15, 80 and 95 (absence, at risk, nomophobia). In the Spanish NMP-Q these are 34, 72 and 94.
- Intervention is underdeveloped: the authors know of no programme that specifically addresses nomophobia. Supports the gap a wellbeing app could fill.
