# almamun2025: The prevalence of nomophobia: A systematic review and meta-analysis

**Authors / venue / year:** Al-Mamun, Mamun, Kaggwa, Mubarak, Hossain, ALmerab, Muhit, Gozal, Griffiths & Sikder / Psychiatry Research 349, article 116521 / 2025
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2025.116521 (free accepted manuscript: https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/53542/1/2436701_Griffiths.pdf)
**Relevance:** medium

> Skimmed from the accepted manuscript; no page numbers.

## Summary (3 sentences)

Meta-analysis of nomophobia prevalence: 43 cross-sectional studies, about 36,000 people, 18 countries, almost all using the NMP-Q (yildirim2015). Pooled "prevalence" is 94%: 26% mild, 51% moderate, 21% severe. Highest in university students (27% severe) and young adults; no real difference between countries or income levels.

## Method

- Ovid databases (Embase, PsycInfo, Medline…), PubMed, Web of Science, to December 2022; English only. PROSPERO registered.
- Only studies with a validated nomophobia scale; studies that measured "nomophobia" with PSU scales were excluded. 42 of 43 used the NMP-Q.
- Severity bands for the NMP-Q (total 20–140): 21–59 mild, 60–99 moderate, 100–140 severe.
- Random-effects pooling; subgroups by country, income level, population, instrument, sampling; meta-regression on sample size and quality; publication bias with Egger, trim-and-fill, PET-PEESE.
- 14 of 43 studies from India; most from low- and middle-income countries.

## Results / evidence

- Any nomophobia 94% (90–99%), I² = 95%. Mild 26%, moderate 51%, severe 21%.
- Population: severe nomophobia highest in university students (27%), then medical students (16%), adults and adolescents (14%), young adults (13%). Adolescents had the most mild, young adults the most moderate.
- Country (India 93%, Turkey 94%, Saudi Arabia 78%) and income level (92–95%): no significant difference.
- Sampling method changes the result: snowball 99%, purposive 88%.
- Publication bias present; after correction overall 83–91%, mild ~20%, moderate ~44–48%, severe ~18%.

## Strengths / limitations

- Strength: only validated scales; reports severity, not just "yes/no".
- **Limitation: 94% is not a meaningful prevalence.** Anyone scoring 21 or more out of 140 on the NMP-Q counts as "mild" (the minimum score is 20), so nearly everyone qualifies. Only "severe" (~20%) says much. The authors admit these are "self-reported levels of distress rather than clinical diagnoses".
- Limitation: the severity bands are not validated against any clinical judgement (yildirim2015 set no cut-off).
- Limitation: extreme heterogeneity (I² up to 99%), publication bias, English only, all cross-sectional.
- Limitation: skewed toward India and student samples; few high-income countries.
- Limitation: sloppy numbers (36,656 vs 36,356 participants; severe 21% vs 22%).

## How it relates to my thesis

Shows that nomophobia scores are high everywhere and highest in university students and young adults, likely the target users of an Android wellbeing app. But it should be cited as "about 1 in 5 report severe nomophobia on the NMP-Q", not "94% have nomophobia".
