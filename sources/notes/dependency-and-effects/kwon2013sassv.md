# kwon2013sassv: The Smartphone Addiction Scale: Development and Validation of a Short Version for Adolescents

**Authors / venue / year:** Kwon, Kim, Cho & Yang / PLOS ONE 8(12), e83558 / 2013
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0083558
**Relevance:** high (the SAS-SV is the most used PSU scale; olson2022's 24-country comparison is in SAS-SV scores; ~2,200 citations on OpenAlex)

## Summary (3 sentences)

Shortens the 33-item SAS (kwon2013sas) to 10 items for adolescents. It is the first version with a cut-off for "addiction", set separately for boys (31) and girls (33) by comparing scores with clinical psychologists' assessments. It answers some limitations of the original SAS (adults only, no cut-off, gender) but still rests on a small, local sample.

## Method

- 540 second-year junior high school students (mean age 14.5) from two schools in Kangwon province, South Korea, April–May 2013. 343 boys, 197 girls.
- Item selection: 7 experts (3 psychiatrists, 2 nursing PhDs, psychologists) rated the relevance of the 33 SAS items (content validity index). Items rated relevant by at least 6 experts (CVI ≥ .78) were kept: 10 items.
- Concurrent validity: correlation with the full SAS, the SAPS (Korean smartphone addiction proneness scale) and the KS-scale (Internet addiction).
- Cut-off: 150 students (90 boys, 60 girls) randomly selected and assessed by clinical psychologists for tolerance, withdrawal and daily-life disturbance; ROC analysis against that judgement.
- 6-point Likert; total 10–60.

## Results / evidence

- The 10 items: missing planned work; trouble concentrating; wrist or neck pain; can't stand not having the phone; impatient and fretful without it; phone on the mind when not using it; won't give up use even when life is affected; checking to not miss SNS conversations; using longer than intended; people around me say I use it too much (Table 3, p. 5).
- Cronbach's alpha .91.
- Correlation with full SAS r = .96, SAPS .76, KS-scale (Internet addiction) .42.
- Girls score higher than boys (27.9 vs 23.8). Self-rated "addicted" students score 34 vs 22 for "not addicted".
- Clinical psychologists judged 15 of 90 boys (16.6%) and 16 of 60 girls (26.6%) as addicted.
- Cut-offs: boys ≥ 31 (AUC .963, sensitivity .87, specificity .89, PPV 62%, NPV 97%); girls ≥ 33 (AUC .947, sensitivity .88, specificity .89, PPV 74%, NPV 95%).
- Use: 3.0 h/day weekdays, 4.1 h weekends; 56% mainly messenger/SNS.

## Strengths / limitations

- Strength: short and easy; first SAS version with a cut-off, and the cut-off is anchored to clinical psychologists' judgement, not only statistics.
- Strength: separate cut-offs by gender, since girls score higher.
- Limitation: the gender issue is handled with separate cut-offs, not a balanced sample; the sample is now 64% boys. The authors admit this.
- Limitation: the cut-offs rest on 31 "addicted" students (15 boys, 16 girls), and were set and tested on the same sample with no independent validation, so accuracy is probably overestimated.
- Limitation: PPV 62% for boys: about 4 in 10 boys above the cut-off were not judged addicted. Supports panova2018's false-positive point.
- Limitation: the clinical "diagnosis" criteria (tolerance, withdrawal, daily-life disturbance) are not described in detail and were not a structured interview.
- Limitation: r = .96 with the full SAS is expected, since the 10 items are part of it and were answered in the same questionnaire.
- Limitation: one region, one school year, 14-year-olds only, but the scale and cut-offs are now widely used with adults (e.g. olson2022, ages 15–35).
- Limitation: contradicts itself: offers a "cut-off value for diagnosis" but also says the SAS "is not designed to pathologically diagnose smartphone addiction but more to identify the level of the smartphone addiction risk" (p. 7).

## How it relates to my thesis

If the thesis cites PSU prevalence or uses a PSU measure, this is most likely the scale behind it. It should be described as a screening tool for risk, not a diagnosis, and its cut-offs were set on Korean 14-year-olds.
