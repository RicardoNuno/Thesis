# olson2022: Smartphone addiction is increasing across the world: A meta-analysis of 24 countries

**Authors / venue / year:** Olson, Sandra, Colucci, Al Bikaii, Chmoulevitch, Nahas, Raz & Veissière / Computers in Human Behavior 129, article 107138 / 2022
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2021.107138
**Relevance:** high

## Summary (3 sentences)

A meta-analysis of studies using the Smartphone Addiction Scale (SAS) across 24 countries. Problematic smartphone use (PSU) is a problem across the world, and SAS scores have been rising over the years. The trend is expected to keep rising.

## Method

- Meta-analysis of studies using the SAS / SAS-SV.
- 24 countries, 83 samples, 33,831 participants, 2014–2020.
- Country-level comparison of SAS scores against cultural tightness/looseness and smartphone integration (ownership, internet time).

## Results / evidence

- SAS scores increased over the years (r = .39 across samples).
- Country explains 74% of the variation. Highest scores: China and Saudi Arabia, then Malaysia, Brazil, South Korea, Iran, Canada, Turkey. Lowest: Germany and France.
- Culturally looser countries report lower SAS scores (r = −.56, 17 countries, exploratory). Proposed reason: tighter, collectivist cultures have stronger social obligations to stay in contact.
- Smartphone ownership and smartphone internet time show only non-linear relationships with SAS; the authors say more countries are needed to confirm them. So ownership does not explain the looseness result.
- The authors expect the rise to continue.

## Strengths / limitations

- Strengths: large multi-country sample, one common scale, so scores are comparable across countries and years.
- Limitations: SAS is self-reported. Country-level correlations do not show causes at the individual level.

## How it relates to my thesis

Supports the "why now": PSU is a worldwide problem and it is growing, not shrinking. Uses the term "addiction"; see panova2018 for why that framing is contested.
