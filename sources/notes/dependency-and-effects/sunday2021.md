# sunday2021: The effects of smartphone addiction on learning: A meta-analysis

**Authors / venue / year:** Sunday, Adesope & Maarhuis / Computers in Human Behavior Reports 4, article 100114 / 2021
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2021.100114
**Relevance:** medium

## Summary (3 sentences)

A meta-analysis of how smartphone addiction relates to learning and academic performance. People with more problematic use learn worse, but the effect is small (r = −.12). The more the phone is used while studying, especially multitasking in class, the stronger the negative effect.

## Method

- Meta-analysis; correlations (r) converted to Fisher's Z; fixed- and random-effects models.
- PsycINFO, ERIC, PubMed, Web of Science, Google Scholar and others. 320 records, 44 studies (45 effects).
- 147,943 students (mostly university; K-12 too) from 16 countries.
- Outcomes: GPA, test scores, SAT/GRE, class scores, cognitive tasks.
- Broad definition of "addiction": includes problematic use, compulsive use, excessive use, frequency of use and multitasking in class.
- Moderators: source, continent, grade level, purpose of use, GPA predictor, construct, test format, design, instrument.

## Results / evidence

All correlations are negative (more phone use, worse learning).

- Overall: r = −.12 (random effects), 95% CI −.08 to −.17; fixed effects r = −.06. Very high heterogeneity (I² = 94%).
- Purpose of use: video gaming r = −.17, texting and socialising −.13, talking and chatting −.10, text messaging −.06; entertainment not significant.
- GPA predictor: multitasking in class r = −.16 (strongest), time on phone −.10, excessive texting −.06, excessive use −.06.
- Construct: smartphone addiction, dependency and frequency of use all about −.06 to −.08, no difference between them.
- Continent: Africa −.20, South America −.14, North America −.07, Europe −.06, Asia −.05 (Africa and South America only 2 studies each).
- Design: correlational studies r = −.06 significant; experimental studies r = −.03 not significant.
- No publication bias (fail-safe N = 2787; Egger's test).

## Strengths / limitations

- Strength: large sample, many countries, outcomes include actual grades and tests, not only self-rated performance.
- Limitation: the effect is small (r = −.12 explains about 1.4% of the variance in learning).
- Limitation: the title says "effects", but the significant results come from correlational studies; the experimental subgroup is not significant. So it does not show that addiction causes worse learning.
- Limitation: "addiction" is defined so broadly (frequency of use, multitasking) that it covers normal heavy use too.
- Limitation: very high heterogeneity; some subgroups rest on 1–2 studies.

## How it relates to my thesis

Adds a further cost of PSU beyond mental health and sleep: worse learning and academic performance, strongest when the phone is used during study or class.
