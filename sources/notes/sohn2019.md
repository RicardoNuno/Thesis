# sohn2019: Prevalence of problematic smartphone usage and associated mental health outcomes amongst children and young people: a systematic review, meta-analysis and GRADE of the evidence

**Authors / venue / year:** Sohn, Rees, Wildridge, Kalk & Carter / BMC Psychiatry 19, article 356 / 2019
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-019-2350-x
**Relevance:** high

## Summary (3 sentences)

A systematic review and meta-analysis of studies on how problematic smartphone use (PSU) relates to mental health problems in children and young people. About one in four young people show PSU. PSU is associated with higher odds of depression, anxiety, stress and poor sleep.

## Method

- Systematic review and meta-analysis, evidence quality rated with GRADE.
- 8 databases, studies from January 2011 to October 2017, no language restriction.
- 924 studies found, 41 included: 38 cross-sectional, 3 cohort.
- 41,871 children and young people, 55% female.
- Outcomes (all self-reported): depression, anxiety, stress, poor sleep quality, decreased educational attainment.

## Results / evidence

- Median PSU prevalence: 23.3% (14.0–31.2%).
- Depression: OR 3.17 (95% CI 2.30–4.37).
- Anxiety: OR 3.05 (95% CI 2.64–3.53).
- Perceived stress: OR 1.86 (95% CI 1.24–2.77).
- Poor sleep quality: OR 2.60 (95% CI 1.39–4.85).
- Educational attainment: no pooled result in the abstract. Check the full text before claiming an effect on studies.

## Strengths / limitations

- Strengths: large pooled sample, pre-registered protocol, GRADE quality rating.
- Limitations: 38 of 41 studies are cross-sectional, so it shows association, not causation (PSU could cause poor mental health or the other way round). All measures are self-reported. High heterogeneity for depression and sleep (I² = 78%).
- Covers children and young people only.
- Has two published errata (2019, 2021).

## How it relates to my thesis

Shows that problematic smartphone use is common and goes together with mental health problems, so reducing and managing how much people depend on the phone is a worthwhile goal. Uses the term "problematic smartphone use" rather than "addiction"; see panova2018 for why the addiction framing is contested.
