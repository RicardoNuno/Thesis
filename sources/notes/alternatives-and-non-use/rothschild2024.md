# rothschild2024: Swapping 5G for 3G: Motivations, Experiences, and Implications of Contemporary Dumbphone Adoption

**Authors / venue / year:** Rothschild & Lindqvist / Proc. ACM Hum.-Comput. Interact. 8 (CSCW1), Article 125, 40 pages / 2024
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3637402 (open access)
**Relevance:** medium

## Summary (3 sentences)

Interview study of 13 people in wealthy, smartphone-saturated countries who chose dumbphones (8 traditional, e.g. Nokia/KaiOS; 5 "designer" dumbphones such as Light Phone II, Punkt., Mudita Pure) plus 2 designer-dumbphone makers. Motivations fell into three groups: ideology (privacy, politics, religion, e.g. Kosher phones), protecting attention from a smartphone experienced as a "hungry monster" and an "itch in your pocket", and simple ambivalence; experiences of disconnection ranged from dangerous (no ride-hailing after a hospital discharge, banking MFA tied to smartphone apps) to welcome (work-life boundaries, more presence with children). Users kept their lives working by offloading smartphone functions onto other people and extra devices (camera, GPS, music player, backup smartphone), which the authors frame with Actor-Network Theory and as a matter of privilege.

## Method

- Semi-structured Zoom interviews (39–80 min, mean 55, 811 min total), with experiences reaching into the COVID-19 pandemic; €20 gift card.
- Recruited from r/dumbphones and r/digitalminimalism plus snowball sampling; 30 eligible, 22 invited, 13 user interviews kept. Makers recruited from the "dumbphone finder" list (2 of 4 contacted).
- Participants: 8 countries, aged 19–51, 13 men and 2 women, highly educated, ~70% technically savvy; most isolated as the only dumbphone user in their circle.
- Grounded-theory style open coding and thematic analysis; ANT applied after the fact. No counts reported by design.

## Results / evidence

- Motivations: (1) ideological: social media and Big Tech disaffiliation, privacy for children, religious observance; (2) attention and health: smartphone as "a hungry monster" (U3-27), "itch in your pocket" (U5-28), doomscrolling, missing time with family and books; (3) ambivalence and nostalgia: no rabbit holes without mobile data.
- Device choice driven by materiality (durability, battery, T9 keypad, size) and messaging: group SMS threading (US) or WhatsApp/Signal (elsewhere); KaiOS seen as buggy and aimed at people who cannot afford smartphones, which pushed some to expensive designer phones (€300–370 vs ~€9 for a Nokia 105).
- Makers' customers: parents (for themselves or their children, avoiding cameras and sexting), people recovering from pornography addiction (no image display), construction workers (durability), Haredi communities (phone must not "look cool"), people avoiding state surveillance during COVID tracing.
- Disconnection experiences: negative (ride-hailing, EU banking MFA via apps solved with an Android emulator, COVID test links, app-only door codes, workplace expectations), positive (no camera means being present at a child's swim practice, conversations with strangers at the dog park, unreachable after work hours), neutral (no instant search, waiting at restaurants).
- Communication: lost group chats and links; some felt broadcast-style smartphone messaging was "broadcasting more than dialogue"; networks shrank to fewer, stronger ties; fear of missing out faded within a week or two for one participant.
- Offloading: borrowing a partner's phone, asking strangers to email photos, carrying iPod, camera, GPS, watch, or a backup smartphone; one replaced a work smartphone with a cellular Apple Watch because it "does not invite endless interaction".

## Strengths / limitations

- Strength: rare look at people who reject smartphones despite access, including designer-device makers, and at the practical infrastructure that assumes a smartphone.
- Strength: honest about privilege: opting out requires money, technical skill and social tolerance.
- Limitation: 13 users, 13 of 15 interviewees men, recruited from Reddit enthusiast communities; highly educated and tech-savvy, so not representative.
- Limitation: qualitative only, with no counts, no usage data and no wellbeing measures; benefits are self-described.
- Limitation: the ANT framing is applied after the interviews and adds vocabulary more than findings.
- Limitation: several participants switched between dumbphones and smartphones, and long-term effects (e.g. loss of weak ties) are untested.

## How it relates to my thesis

Shows the far end of the spectrum of responses to smartphone overuse: replacing the device rather than regulating it, and what that costs in a society built around smartphones (banking MFA, ride-hailing, health services, group chats). That cost is an argument for interventions that keep the smartphone but reduce its pull, which is the space my proposal works in. Participants' reasons (attention, constant availability, doomscrolling) match the problems that interventions target (cf. tran2019, cho2021), and the makers' design choices (no images, no "cool" interactions, limited apps) are hardware versions of friction and feature removal (cf. datta2022, lu2024, olson2022nudge's greyscale). It also notes the smartwatch as a lower-pull alternative, relevant to the smartwatch section of the reading list.
