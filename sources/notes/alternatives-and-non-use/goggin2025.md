# goggin2025: Mobile AI: Communication and Mobility After the Smartphone

**Authors / venue / year:** Gerard Goggin / Communication and Change 1, Article 5 / 2025
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s44382-025-00002-3 (open access)
**Relevance:** low

## Summary (3 sentences)

Critical media-studies essay on "mobile AI" in 2022–2025 as a site where futures "after the smartphone" are imagined, using case studies from marketing, launch events and press: incumbents adding generative AI to phones (Qualcomm, Samsung Galaxy AI, Apple Intelligence) and new dedicated "AI devices" (Humane ai pin, Rabbit R1, the rumoured Jony Ive–Sam Altman device). Goggin argues AI will push the smartphone further from being a "phone" and that these devices promise a less screen-bound, more seamless experience, but their imaginaries are still inchoate and echo past promises (mobile internet, 5G). He then sets this against global inequalities in phone ownership and 5G access, and closes with a ten-question research agenda (practices, political economy, inclusion, language, environment, governance).

## Method

- Conceptual essay with illustrative cases drawn from company blogs, ads, keynotes, product pages and tech journalism; framed by long histories of new media, political economy of telecoms and AI, and smartphone scholarship. No empirical study.

## Results / evidence

- Incumbents: on-device generative AI pitched as convenience, personalisation, accessibility and privacy (data kept "at the edge"); live call translation is the headline feature (Samsung Live Translate, up to 13 languages at launch). Goggin doubts the privacy claims given smartphones' "leaky" data architectures.
- AI devices: Humane ai pin ("See the world, not your screen"; projected laser display, "trust light", USD 499–699, poor reviews, charging-case recall); Rabbit R1 (USD 199, agents operating apps for you, "Large Action Model", >100,000 sold by Aug 2024, called "half-baked"); founders explicitly frame them as companions rather than phone replacements ("You go [to your phone] to waste your time ... But you go [to the R1] to save your time").
- Ive–Altman project: reported aim of "a computing experience that is less socially disruptive than the iPhone"; still speculative at time of writing.
- Context: 78% of people aged 10+ own a mobile phone (47% in low-income groups vs 94% high-income); GSMA 2023: 5.6 billion unique subscribers, 78% of connections smartphones, 5G only 18% of connections.

## Strengths / limitations

- Strength: broad, well-referenced map of the "post-smartphone" device landscape and the rhetoric around it, with attention to inequality and environment.
- Limitation: relies on marketing and press; no users or data; devices assessed by claims and reviews.
- Limitation: factual slips: dates the ai pin's Paris Fashion Week launch to October 2024 (it was 2023, before shipping in April 2024), and calls LoveFrom "Altman's firm" (it is Ive's, with Marc Newson, spelled "Newsom").
- Limitation: written late 2024; events since (e.g. Humane's ai pin being discontinued in early 2025) date some cases.

## How it relates to my thesis

Background for the "beyond the smartphone" framing and any smartphone-substitute discussion: the AI devices are pitched as reducing screen time and phone dependence ("See the world, not your screen"), the same promise as minimal phones (lee2026, jansson2024) and the smartwatch-as-substitute idea noted in rosenberg2022. It lists smartwatches, wearables and glasses as long-standing alternatives to the phone, but gives no evidence that any of them reduce overuse. Useful as a cautionary note that "post-smartphone" devices are marketing imaginaries more than proven alternatives.
