# lee2026: Going Light: The Effects of Minimal Mobile Phone Adoption on Young Adults' Well-Being Depend on Motivation

**Authors / venue / year:** Lee, Stevic, Walker-Keleher, Chen, Charity, Dahlke & Hancock / CHI '26 / 2026
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791723 (preprint: https://osf.io/ubkeq)
**Relevance:** medium

_Skimmed from the PsyArXiv preprint (Feb 2026), so wording and numbers may differ slightly from the published version; quotes cite sections._

## Summary (3 sentences)

US students swapped their smartphone for a Light Phone II (E-Ink, calls, texts, GPS, alarm, music; no social media, browser, email, camera or app store) for one week, in three groups: high-interest volunteers, students randomly assigned to the Light Phone, and students randomly assigned to keep their smartphone. Using the Light Phone cut self-reported phone and social media use and raised perceived agency over phone use, though both partly rebounded during the week. Well-being improved only for the volunteers (less stress, higher life satisfaction); students assigned to the phone showed no well-being gains and even reported more phone dependency at first, so the authors conclude that effects depend on motivation.

## Method

- Quasi-experiment, April–November 2024, one US university. Volunteers recruited by flyers/emails ("high-interest"); a social-studies class randomised into Light Phone ("treatment") or own smartphone ("control").
- Research assistants moved SIM cards to the Light Phone and set up contacts; participants were asked not to use their smartphone and to submit an iOS Screen Time / Android Digital Wellbeing screenshot at the end for compliance; they could quit at any time.
- Four surveys over the week (T1 baseline, T2–T4 during use): self-reported phone and social media use frequency, phone dependency, agency and valence mindsets, PANAS (positive affect, productivity), social connection, DASS depression and stress, life satisfaction; TIPI personality at baseline.
- Pre-registered hypotheses; entropy balancing to reweight groups on demographics and personality; multilevel linear and quadratic growth models, weighted and unweighted.
- Sample: 209 enrolled, 156 completed all waves; 21 years old on average, 54% women, 139 iPhone users.

## Results / evidence

- Phone and social media use: steep drop then partial rebound for Light Phone users; the assigned group dropped more sharply than volunteers (volunteers already used social media less at baseline).
- Dependency: down overall, driven by volunteers (steep drop, some rebound); the assigned group reported *more* dependency early on, easing later.
- Perceived agency: rose for Light Phone users, mainly volunteers, and plateaued. The assigned group grew more negative about phones in general.
- No effect on positive affect, productivity, social connection or depression; social connection did not fall, contrary to the hypothesis.
- Stress fell and life satisfaction rose only in the volunteer group; no change for the assigned group.
- Authors describe three phases (initiation, adaptation, stabilisation) and suggest support should be "back-loaded" for when enthusiasm wanes.

## Strengths / limitations

- Strength: first controlled test of a minimal phone, with a randomised arm plus a self-selected arm, which shows directly how motivation changes outcomes.
- Strength: pre-registered, balancing and robustness checks, several dimensions of well-being.
- Limitation: severe attrition in the assigned group: 52 started and only 15 completed all four surveys, so the "unmotivated" comparison rests on a small, probably selective group.
- Limitation: one week; use and well-being are self-reported (screenshots were only a compliance check, not analysed as usage data).
- Limitation: groups come from different recruitment channels and differ at baseline; balancing covers only demographics and personality.
- Limitation: inconsistencies: the abstract says n = 166 and mentions less FoMO, but the methods report 156 completers and the results never report FoMO; the text says dependency fell "for all users" while the assigned group's dependency rose; hypothesis numbering repeats (two H3s).
- Limitation: almost all iPhone users at one US residential university.

## How it relates to my thesis

Complements rothschild2024 with a controlled test of the "replace the phone" approach: removing features by design reduces use and raises agency, but only motivated people gain in well-being, and use rebounds within a week. That supports measuring motivation as a moderator in any evaluation (cf. hiniker2016, parry2023, mark2018) and argues against assuming that a stronger constraint is better for everyone. The authors place Digital Wellbeing and Screen Time limits as the light-touch middle of a "disconnection continuum" between friction apps like one sec (haliburton2024) and full detox, which is a useful framing for where an Android prototype sits. The early rise in dependency among people forced into the change echoes the reactance and abandonment seen with restrictive interventions (kim2019goalkeeper, lyngs2019).
