# jansson2024: Resonance in an accelerating world: Understanding the emergence and characteristics of post-digital artifacts

**Authors / venue / year:** Jansson & Thorén / Convergence 32(4), 1338–1359 / 2024 (issue 2026)
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1177/13548565241302246 (open access; also https://uu.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1912729/FULLTEXT01)
**Relevance:** low

_Skimmed from the open-access online-first version; quotes cite sections._

## Summary (3 sentences)

Qualitative content analysis of the websites and marketing of four "post-digital artifacts" that sell limited functionality as a feature: the Light Phone II, the Punkt MP02 phone, the reMarkable paper tablet and the Freewrite typewriter. Using Rosa's concepts of alienation and resonance, the authors derive five "trajectories" the products promise, from complexity to simplicity, distraction to focus, artificiality to authenticity, immateriality to materiality, and passivity to activity. They conclude that these products foster technological awareness and a reclaiming of control over time and space, but rest on a paradox: freedom from technology is sold as more technology to buy.

## Method

- Explorative case study with typical-case sampling: products whose webpages explicitly mention distraction, focus or information stress and a desire to escape screens or social media.
- Data: visual, textual and audio material on each product's main website and crowdfunding pages, read as both advertising and a mirror of social discourse.
- Two rounds of coding (open in vivo codes such as "digital is distracting", "too much choice"; then themes mapped to resonance vs alienation), producing a table of five dichotomies.

## Results / evidence

- Simplicity: few features, no apps, no data traces; "a simple phone that actually respects you" (Light).
- Focus: no feeds or notifications; the device acts as "a kind of mute button" ("It will never have social media, clickbait news, email, an internet browser, or any other anxiety-inducing infinite feed", Light Phone II).
- Authenticity: marketing shows family, friends and nature, with the product nearly absent, "as if it evaporates upon purchase"; what is sold is absence.
- Materiality: E-Ink "paper" screens, physical buttons, the feel of keys.
- Activity: flow and creativity instead of passive consumption.
- All four companies are small and mostly crowdfunded, positioning themselves against Silicon Valley yet dependent on it to exist.
- Resonance is defined here as "the slow, tactile friction of an imperfect ... human agency"; alienation as loss of tactility and agency amid complex, opaque systems.

## Strengths / limitations

- Strength: names the design values these products sell and the solutionism paradox clearly.
- Limitation: analyses marketing material only, so it shows what companies claim, not what users experience or whether the products work (the authors say this is not the focus).
- Limitation: four products picked by the authors; theory-heavy (Rosa, Foucault, practice theory) with little systematic reporting of the coding.
- Limitation: repeats marketing statistics (e.g., 25 minutes to refocus, phone checked every 12 minutes) as quoted, without checking them.

## How it relates to my thesis

Background only. It explains the design rhetoric of the dedicated minimal devices studied empirically in lee2026 (Light Phone II) and rothschild2024 (designer dumbphones): simplicity, removing feeds and notifications, and deliberate friction. Its "mute button" and friction-as-agency framing parallel interventions on a normal smartphone (cf. schraggeova2025's text launcher, datta2022's feature removal). The solutionism paradox, buying technology to escape technology, applies equally to wellbeing apps (cf. parry2025, vanvelthoven2018's "irony").
